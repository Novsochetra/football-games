# MERIDIAN THREAD: shot spec

No.10's special pass. Head up, eyes on the far side, he makes the whole back line believe the ball
is going one way. Then, without looking, he plays it with the outside of his right boot. The ball
rolls low across the grass, trailing a taut jade thread. It slips through three needle-eye gaps,
bends round the last defender's heel and arrives in No.7's stride. From the moment No.10 reads the
play, the stadium drains into a manga page of ink and paper. Only the two red shirts, the ball and the
thread keep their colour. When No.7's first touch pulls the thread tight, the colour washes back
along it, and he scores.

This is No.10's first **special pass**. ZENITH METEOR (`../zenith/`) is the quality bar and CRIMSON
CRESCENT (`../crescent/SPEC.md`) is the format. MERIDIAN THREAD must feel like their sibling: same
engine, same pixel-art and post-FX look, same polish. But it is a **pass**, a clear notch below
every shot in raw power:
- no fire, explosions, shock rings or sky;
- nobody is blasted: defenders are beaten by the disguise and their own momentum;
- the spectacle comes from precision, freeze frames, music, the look-off gag and the colour return.

Its personality is the cheeky, generous playmaker who makes his friend the hero. That is the
teamwork message for young players.

**Where it came from.** This brief starts from the MERIDIAN THREAD pitch (pitch C) and changes it:
- **Recoloured** from cyan to **jade / emerald + pearl on ink and paper**. Cyan, cobalt and ice belong
  to GALE HAMMER.
- **Every ring removed**: the plant ring, the flat ring at the strike, the refraction shocks, the target
  ring and the circular colour return. The target is a **cross-stitch X**, and the colour washes back
  **along the thread**.
- **The ball rolls.** It is a true ground pass and never skips or bounces. No water, ripples or wakes.
- **The motif is retuned** to a D-pentatonic plucked figure (E–F#–A, resolving up to D). It no longer
  shadows GALE HAMMER's rising G–B–D air tones.
- **No.7 is studio-drawn** for his run, receiving touch, finish and point. The one-touch finish
  becomes a **receive in stride, then finish**.

**Set-critic fixes** (Nov 2026; these keep the five specials apart, see the identity table):
- **The ball scene is a manga page**, not a tracking side rail: GALE HAMMER owns the grass-level
  rail that holds the ball at the left third. Shot 4 is now three locked panels that the ball
  crosses, with a page-pan between them (the move's own camera language).
- **D1 poke-tackles** instead of sliding: the slide tackle is SPOTLIGHT SOLO's money beat (GALE
  HAMMER's defender now jump-blocks). One slide tackle in the whole set.
- **No shader impact frame.** A whole-frame black/white impact is ZENITH METEOR's; the HIT is now a
  canvas **ink freeze** where the world goes to ink and paper and No.10 and the ball stay in colour.
- **Nothing splits into sliding halves** (CRIMSON CRESCENT's slash split): the cut-in panels are
  pulled off the page after the needle, and the thread wipe is a straight, taut wipe edge.
- **The crowd is only dipped, never silenced** at the read (`duck(0.35, …)`): silence and muffling
  are SPOTLIGHT SOLO's and GALE HAMMER's. His watch ticks cut through the noise.
- **No.10 is never the puppet, in the result view too**: he celebrates from studio frames.
- The pass now owns the **rising note per ball event** outright: GALE HAMMER's steps are pitchless.

Grafts from the other two pitches, kept only where they fit the thread and ink identity:
- from A: the motif previewed in the command window, the plain ball on No.7's finish ("it is No.10's
  special"), and the ball as the brightest point with a dark halo;
- from B: the chin-dip "decision" glance, and the symmetrical frame with the ball alone between two
  defenders (encounter 2).

**Originality guard**
- The name was checked by web search (Nov 2026): no anime or game move is called "Meridian Thread".
  The nearest names are "Meridian Sweep" (Mabinogi) and "Meridian Touch" (Where Winds Meet). Re-check
  before shipping.
- It is not a Captain Tsubasa move. Never use the word "drive" (names, captions, code). It is not a
  one-two combination. The ball never knocks a defender down: it beats them by missing them.

### MERIDIAN THREAD owned motifs and palette

**Motifs this move owns.** No other skill may use these as its core look:
- the jade/emerald + pearl palette;
- the ink-page grade and the colour washing back along a line;
- threads, strings and running stitches;
- vesica needle eyes and the cross-stitch X target;
- multi-panel manga page layouts: ink borders, paper gutters, panel freezes, the diptych;
- the plucked-string motif;
- the watch-tick inner clock;
- the thread-wipe transition;
- the hand-lettered ink SFX (`SHK!`, `KLAK!`, `SWSH`);
- a rising plucked note on each ball event.

**Never used here**:
- `fx.shocks`, `ring()`, `PK.Ring` or any circle or ellipse ring;
- flames, `fireRibbon`, `crimsonRibbon`, `crescentArc` / `crescentPath`, moons and sky shots;
- camera roll (none at all: the page's panels are slanted 2–4°, which is layout, not a roll);
- the slash split or any frame split into sliding halves, the pressure lens, the muffle low-pass;
- the `fx.impact` shader (a whole-frame two-tone impact is ZENITH METEOR's black/white);
- a tracking side rail that holds the ball at the left third (GALE HAMMER's ball scene);
- slide tackles (SPOTLIGHT SOLO's money beat);
- heartbeats (CRIMSON CRESCENT), sonic booms (GALE HAMMER), drums and crowd chants (SPOTLIGHT SOLO);
- a crowd duck deeper than 0.6, or any full silence;
- `timeStop()`, `roar()`, `stinger()` at the cut-in;
- spotlights, dimmed floodlights and afterimages;
- cyan, aqua, cobalt or violet energy, and `lensFlare` at its default (cyan) tint: always pass a
  jade tint such as `[150,255,200]`.

- Jade (hue about 150°) is clearly greener than the rivals' teal trim and boots (`#28BEAA`, hue about
  172°). The rivals are inked with the page anyway, so the two never sit side by side in colour.
- Every jade glow has an **ink under-stroke** (normal blend) under it, which is also the manga
  outline. So it never blows out on paper, and still reads on green grass once colour returns.
- Ball after the strike: `drawBall(..., 'pearl')`. It is a normal white ball with a pearl core glow,
  an emerald outer glow and its 1 px outline. There is no heat: it is a pass, not a fireball.
- Particle ramps (new): `'jade'` = `[255,252,240] > [190,255,215] > [47,224,138] > [11,143,88] > [13,40,28]`
  and `'ink'` = `[239,231,214] > [160,152,140] > [60,58,62] > [13,13,20]`.
- Post settings for every shot:
  - `bloom` 0.7 and `bloomThresh` 0.72, so paper whites do not bloom;
  - `contrast` 1.08 in inked shots;
  - `grain` 0.05 (paper grain);
  - `sat` stays 1, because the grade is done on the canvas.
- `fx.flash` colour is always paper `[239,231,214]`, never pure white.

## Continuity (all shots)

**Setting and direction**
- Night match under floodlights. The goal is to the **right** (+x). Field scale: 10 px ≈ 1 m, goal line
  at x 1180, far post z 214, near post z 300.
- No.10 is right-footed and runs east. Studio Side frames are drawn facing east and seen from the near
  (south) touchline, so the **near leg is his right, kicking leg**.
- **His left is the far side** (low z, up-screen in field views). **His right is the near side** (high
  z), where No.7 runs.
- Over his shoulder (shot 2, Back view), the far side is screen-left and No.7 comes from screen-right.

**The pass, as real football**
- He sells the far side with his eyes and head. The ball sets off slightly toward the far side and
  bends right, behind the line, into No.7.
- Outside of the right boot: the standing foot is planted **further from the ball than usual** and a
  little behind it. The ankle is locked, toes pointed down and in. The outside of the boot brushes
  forward across the **inside-back** of the ball (the half nearest the standing foot).
- That gives clockwise spin seen from above, so the ball bends to his right. The kicking leg finishes
  **out to his right**.
- The ball **rolls on the turf**, and never rises more than about 4 px. Its spin reads as sideways
  panel slide plus forward roll.

### 1 · Stitch-in (1.3 s)

Declare the move. It belongs to the same family as the other cut-ins (eyes, glint, title, tag), but
it is a manga page and it is **stitched**, not slammed or swept.

**Background** (screen space)
- Warm paper `#EFE7D6`. Ink speed lines stream diagonally (`speedStreaks` in ink at −18°, alpha 0.35).
- Cached screentone dots (`screentone`) darken the four corners.
- Camera: a slow 3 % push-in under everything.

**Beats**
- **0.00–0.18** two slanted manga panels slam in from opposite sides (`ease.outBack` overshoot) with
  3 px ink borders and a 6 px paper gutter between them. `cam.hit(0.25)`.
  - **Top panel** (about 62 % of the frame height, tilted −6°): No.10's eyes in close-up. This is the
    Glance G1 crop of `art.hero.face` at integer ×4, with jade rim light from screen-right (a tinted
    copy, 1 px offset, additive 0.4) and an ink-wash shadow (multiply).
  - **Bottom panel** (tilted +4°, offset right): the outside of his right boot meeting the ball, a crop
    of Trivela T6 at integer ×4. The ball is drawn at T6's `ball` anchor, with two curved ink spin marks.
- **0.30** the look-off: his pupils flick to his left (G2, screen-right in a front close-up).
  - `sparkle` on the near eye and a small `lensFlare` tinted `[150,255,200]`.
  - The panels shake 1 px.
- **0.42–0.95** the stitch.
  - A pearl needle of light (a 3 px pearl point, emerald halo, short ink streak) enters at screen-left
    and runs one straight line across both panels at y ≈ 205, crossing the gutter.
  - Behind it the thread (ink under-stroke, jade body, pearl core) dips under the paper and comes back
    up every half-letter, like a running stitch. Each dip leaves two 1 px ink needle holes.
  - The title **MERIDIAN THREAD** appears letter by letter exactly as the needle passes. Each letter
    pops from scale 0 to 1 and is pulled 3 px down onto the line.
  - Letter style: ink fill, 2 px jade inner stroke, 3 px paper outer stroke, skew −0.12. This is neither
    Zenith's gold slam, Crescent's crimson sweep nor GALE HAMMER's spring-out.
  - Tag in pixel font, ink: `SPECIAL PASS · No.10 > No.7 · NO-LOOK TRIVELA`.
- **0.98** the thread yanks taut. The letters jerk 2 px onto the line, and the line vibrates like a
  plucked string: a standing wave with 2 nodes, 4 px amplitude, decaying over 0.25 s.
- **1.12–1.30** the needle yanks the thread off the right edge and both panels are **pulled off the
  page after it**, like cloth drawn by a thread (0.12 s, `ease.inCubic`, the bottom panel trailing
  the top by 2 frames). Paper flash (`fx.flash` 0.6), then cut. Nothing splits into halves (that is
  CRIMSON CRESCENT's slash split).

**FX:** `vignette` 0.25.

### 2 · Playmaker's eye (1.8 s)

The read and the disguise. It is quiet and clever, the opposite of a roaring power-up. His inner clock
ticks, and the stadium goes quiet in his head.

- An over-the-shoulder low angle from **behind** No.10, looking downfield at goal.
- Ground: GALE HAMMER's shared perspective pitch `drawDownPitch`, pre-rendered in `prepare()`:

  - converging mowing stripes, box and D lines;
  - a small goal and keeper at the far end;

## Sound design

**Concept: the thread is a string.** The move's voice is a bright plucked string, somewhere between
a harp and a small lute. Every note comes from the **D-major pentatonic** scale, which sits inside the
key of the existing `stinger()` (A major):

| Moment | Notes | Hz |
|---|---|---|
| cut-in opening figure (open fifths, like tuning) | D4, A4, E5 | 293.66, 440.00, 659.26 |
| the strike's low root | D3 | 146.83 |
| eyes 1, 2, 3 | E5, F#5, A5 | 659.26, 739.99, 880.00 |
| No.7's touch, the resolution | D6 | 1174.66 |
| diptych strum | all of the above | |

The figure "mi-fa-sol… DO!" (2-3-5 → 8) is stepwise, then leaps up. It is deliberately not a plain
rising triad, so it never sounds like GALE HAMMER's G–B–D air tones. A child should be able to hum
the pass afterwards.

**Time**
- When No.10 reads the play, `duck(0.35, …)` dips the crowd a little: gain only, never a filter and
  never silence. The stadium noise stays, and his watch ticks cut through it. Full silence is
  SPOTLIGHT SOLO's choke and muffling is GALE HAMMER's mute; this move changes colour, not sound.
- His inner clock is `tick()`, a dry watch click, one per sightline. It is distinct from Crescent's
  heartbeats and the dribble's drums.

**Impacts** are dry and precise, never explosive:
- the strike is `kick` plus the low root pluck, with the ambience sucked out for the hit-stop;
- near-misses are `crack` / `kick` clacks;
- the only `boom` is soft, under the reception.

**Release:** the reverse-swell whoosh as colour returns, then the full crowd, the net, the whistle and
the strum.

**Mix**
- Plucks sit on a 0.6–0.7 reverb send so they ring in the stadium.
- The eye notes climb in level (0.45 → 0.5 → 0.55), and the touch is the loudest pluck.
- Peak loudness stays below GALE HAMMER and Zenith. The existing limiter keeps the strum clean.

**New procedural voices** (`src/engine/audio.ts`)
- **`pluck(freq, decay = 1.2, gain = 0.5)`**:
  - additive partials at 1, 2, 3 and 4.1× `freq` (amplitudes 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.12), with the upper
    partials decaying faster;
  - a 2 ms attack and an exponential decay;
  - a 15-cent sharp onset settling to pitch in 40 ms (string tension);
  - a 3 ms band-passed noise "pick" at 3 kHz.
  - Optional: a soft waveshaper "buzz" so the timbre leans toward a Cambodian chapei. It is a nice
    touch for local kids, not a requirement.
- **`strum(freqs, gap)`**: plucks in sequence.
- **`tick()`**: a 1 ms high-passed noise click plus a 20 ms 3.2 kHz sine blip, dry.
- **`zip(dur = 0.4, f0 = 600, f1 = 4000)`**: band-passed noise (Q 6) sweeping `f0` → `f1`,
  amplitude-modulated by a 70 Hz square: the zipper teeth.
- **`duck(depth, dur)`**: a gain envelope on the crowd bus (30 ms down, hold, 0.25 s release).
  - If GALE HAMMER's `crowdBus` has landed, reuse it. Otherwise add one that `crowd()` connects to.
  - It is driven from cinematic time, so seek and replay are exact.

**Reused:** `boom`, `kick`, `crack`, `whoosh`, `shing`, `crowd`, `strain`, `whistle`, `blip`.
**Not used:** `timeStop`, `roar`, `rumble`, `crackle`, the muffle low-pass, and GALE HAMMER's air
voices.

## Engine cheat-sheet (new pieces; everything else is as in Crescent's sheet)

**The ink page:** `inkGrade(ctx, amount, restore?: { pts, w, soft })` in `src/art/fx.ts`. This is the
signature look and needs no shader change.
- Draw it once per frame, after the environment layer and before the thread, ball and characters.
  Skip it entirely when `amount` = 0.
- Steps:
  1. `'saturation'` fill with mid-grey at `amount`, so the environment turns monochrome;
  2. `'multiply'` fill of `PAL.paper` at `amount`, so the whites go warm paper;
  3. `'multiply'` of the cached screentone pattern at 0.22 × `amount`, for the dot texture.
- **Restore** (the colour washing back along the thread):
  - copy the frame to a scratch canvas and grade the scratch;
  - build the mask by stroking the thread polyline `pts` on a mask canvas: width `w` opaque, plus two
    wider, fainter strokes for the soft edge;
  - cut the mask out of the graded scratch (`destination-out`) and draw the scratch back over the frame.
- Allocate the scratch, mask and screentone pattern once in `prepare()`.
- The post shader stays untouched. Hue-keying would grey the skin and catch the rivals' teal.

**Thread and page helpers** (`./common.ts`, generic ones in `src/art/fx.ts`):
- `threadLine(ctx, pts, w, alpha, opts)`: an ink under-stroke (w + 2), an emerald halo (additive, low
  alpha), a jade body and a pearl core.
  - `opts.stitch` draws a running stitch: 14 px dashes, 6 px dips, ink needle holes.
  - `opts.pluck` is `{ amp, phase, nodes }`: a standing-wave displacement whose nodes sit at the eyes.
- `needleEye(ctx, x, y, len, open, rot, alpha, squash)`: a vesica of two thin arcs that opens like an
  eyelid, with an ink outline and pearl points.
- `crossStitch(ctx, x, y, s, alpha, squash)`: the target X, two stitched strokes with an ink outline.
  It replaces every ring target.
- `stitchTitle(...)`: the running-stitch letter reveal.
- `screentone(ctx, rect, dot, alpha)`: a cached halftone pattern.
- `mangaPanels(ctx, polys, t)`: slanted clip panels with ink borders and paper gutters (the cut-in, the
  insets, the diptych).
- `panelFreeze(ctx, amount)`: the freeze border.
- `inkSfx(ctx, text, x, y, size, rot)`: hand-lettered SFX.
- `spinMarks(ctx, x, y, r, phase)`: two curved manga motion marks on the ball.
- `inkSmear(ctx, pts, w, alpha)`: the boot's swing arc.

**Ground and figures**
- Reuse GALE HAMMER's `drawDownPitch` + `projectDecal` (`src/art/stadium.ts`) for shot 2.
- `drawFrontFigure(ctx, kit, x, y, scale, lean, headTurn, frame)`: small front-view defenders with a
  2-frame shuffle, shared with any skill that needs them.
- `drawGrassFore(ctx, scrollX, smear)`: a pre-rendered strip of grass-blade silhouettes: static in
  shot 4's panel 1, smeared on shot 6's rail behind No.7.
- Reuse `drawDownPitch` for shot 4's panel 2 too (vanishing point at the panel's top centre).

**The page** (shot 4): `PAGE` in `common.ts` holds the three panel polygons in page space
(1280 × 360) and the page-pan keys. `mangaPage(ctx, view, t)` draws the paper, borders and gutters,
clips each live panel, and blits the finished panels from two snapshot canvases allocated in
`prepare()`. Snapshots are rendered deterministically from the panel's end time, so `init` can
rebuild them on a seek.

**Ball:** `BallPalette` gains `'pearl'` (`renderBall` and `drawBall`), with a pearl core glow and an
emerald outer glow over an ink halo. The squash is optional and capped at 10 % here.

**Particles:** new ramps `'jade'` and `'ink'`. `sparks()` accepts them.

**Studio loader:** GALE HAMMER's shared `src/art/studioAnim.ts` (the generalised `heroJump.ts`).
- It loads the No.10 and No.7 strips plus JSON, and draws nearest-neighbour around an anchor.
- Its box-filtered cache at 0.4× / 0.5× is built in `prepare()`, never at a fractional
  nearest-neighbour scale.

**Puppet poses** (defenders and keeper only): `pokeTackle` (new: standing leg planted, the other
swept low across the ball's line, toe reaching), `lungeL`, `lungeR`, `stumble`, `stretchBlock`,
`sprawl`, `sitWrongWay`, `lookFar`. No `slideTackle`: the slide tackle is SPOTLIGHT SOLO's beat.

**Audio:** `pluck`, `strum`, `tick`, `zip`, `duck(depth, dur)` (see Sound design). The crowd bus and
`duck` are shared with GALE HAMMER and SPOTLIGHT SOLO (one signature, depth first). Every shot's
`init` calls the shared `sfx.clearBus()`, even when `silent`, so a seek never leaves the crowd ducked.

**Files:** `src/cinematic/meridian/`
- `index.ts` (`meridianThread`);
- `common.ts` (`PAL`, `LAYOUT`, `PATH`, `SHOT_NAME`, thread helpers);
- `shot1_stitch.ts` … `shot6_touch.ts`;
- this `SPEC.md`.

**Cut-in sizes:** the face crop and the boot crop are drawn at integer ×4 only.

## Quality bar (what reviewers check)

1. **Still frames read instantly:** who, what, which direction. In shot 3's freeze, "eyes here, ball
   there" reads without sound. In shot 5, ball and No.7 meet on the X.
2. **The ball is never lost.**
   - It is at least 20 px across in shot 4 and always the brightest point, with an ink outline and a
     pearl halo.
   - The thread is drawn behind the ball and ends at its trailing edge.
   - Particles fade within about 20 px of the ball.
3. **No long blow-outs.** Paper is bright, so `bloomThresh` stays at 0.72 or above. Every jade glow sits
   on an ink under-stroke (normal blend).
4. **Pixel-art consistency.**
   - No.10 and No.7 come only from studio frames: integer scale, or the box-filtered cache.
   - No.10 is never the puppet.
   - Face crops are drawn at integer ×4 only.
5. **Clean cuts.** Every shot opens and closes on its listed transition: panels pulled off the page,
   paper flash, thread wipe, match cut, fade. Nothing ever splits into sliding halves.
6. **It is clearly MERIDIAN THREAD:**
   - jade, pearl, ink and paper;
   - the thread, needle eyes, stitches and manga panels;
   - the plucked figure.
   It has no rings, water, ripples, skips, cyan, cobalt, violet, spotlights, afterimages, fire, moon,
   sky or camera roll; no tracking side rail in the ball scene, no slide tackle, no shader impact
   frame, no frame split, no silence and no low-pass (see the set rule in the identity section).
7. **Tier discipline.**
   - 6 shots and 10.9 s.
   - Full-frame events are limited to the shot 3 ink freeze, the colour return and GOAL!.
   - Nobody is blasted.
   - Peak loudness stays below the power shots.
8. **Football is right.**
   - The ball rolls, bends right off the outside of the right boot, and the plant foot is wide.
   - No.7 is onside at the strike and receives in stride.
   - Kids could copy the technique.
9. **Performance.**
   - No big canvas allocations per frame.
   - At most 3 puppet renders per frame.
   - Particles under about 1500.
   - `inkGrade` runs once per frame, and its restore path only in shot 6.
   - Shot 4's two panel snapshots are allocated in `prepare()` and rebuilt deterministically on seek.
10. **Photosensitivity** (a game for children): at most 3 full-frame flashes or freezes in any 1 s
    window, across cuts too. The ink freeze's 1-frame negative counts as one. Nothing strobes.

## Risks

1. **Back views and the head turns are the hardest frames**: Scan S2a/S3, Trivela T6 (head away) and
   T8a (face to camera). Draw and review those keys first. Fallbacks are listed in Studio art.
2. **A ground pass is small on screen.** The ink page, the pearl halo with its ink outline, the
   active panel filling about 80 % of the view in shot 4, and the thread always linking the ball to
   No.10 keep it readable. Check stills against bar 1.
3. **Fill rate.** `'saturation'` and `'multiply'` over the full canvas cost fill rate at high DPR. Do it
   once per frame, only in inked shots, and use the scratch copy only in shot 6.
4. **The pass must feel special without power.** If it feels small, lengthen the eye freezes and the
   wink, never add blasts.
5. **Layout agreement.** The over-the-shoulder view, the three page panels and the broadcast view must
   agree on the far and near sides, the D2–D3 gap and the X. Feed everything from `LAYOUT` and
   `PATH`.
6. **The look-off gag is tiny in shot 2.** Exaggerate whole-body leans and the swish marks, and repeat
   the payoff in shot 5 and the result view.
7. **Studio load.** The pass plays 25 library frames for No.10 (most shared or variants) and 20 for
   No.7 (mostly re-skins). Stick to the library's priority order. No.7 also unlocks better assists
   in later skills.
8. **The `'through'` scenario adds two defenders and No.7's run.** Gate them so every other scenario's
   flow is unchanged.
9. **The page must still feel like motion.** Three locked panels can read as slow. Keep the page-pans
   short (0.12 s), let the ball enter and exit each panel at full speed, and keep the thread
   continuous across the gutters so the eye reads one journey. If it still feels static, shorten
   panel 1's lead-in, never turn it back into a tracking rail.
